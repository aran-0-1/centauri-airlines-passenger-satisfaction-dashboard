Centauri Airlines needs to focus on all areas of passenger experience as the average Overall Service Score (OSS) is approximately 3.28, putting it into the 3.01-3.50 band. The satisfaction rate observed within this band is 42%, which is close to the overall satisfaction rate of 43%. The OSS is important, as the observed satisfaction rate increases to 64% in the next band 3.51-4.00. This relationship between OSS and satisfaction rate varies by flight class. For example, the satisfaction rate observed in the next band is 33% for Economy, 43% for Economy Plus and 87% for Business. 

There are some experience areas that fall below this average; for example, Inflight Wi-Fi Service, Online Booking and Food and Drink. There are other experiences that perform below average too; for example, Gate Location and Schedule Convenience. However, these areas may be influenced more by the airport rather than the airline itself. Therefore, the primary focus should be on weaker controllable areas such as the digital experience and catering services.

In general, the top and bottom experience areas are consistent across the three flight classes. The service rating is usually higher in Business, followed by Economy Plus and then Economy. This is crucial, as we discussed earlier that the satisfaction rate among different classes varies greatly. Although the overall satisfaction rate is 43%, this same satisfaction rate drops to 19% for Economy passengers. This is particularly concerning given that Economy passengers account for 45% of the airline’s passenger base. 

While the OSS is consistent across different age groups, there are three age groups that represent 63% of our passenger base. These age groups are: 25-34, 35-44 and 45-54. Therefore, Centauri Airlines may want to focus future improvement efforts that align closely with the behaviour of these age groups. 
Another association that was analysed with satisfaction outcomes was loyalty. Nearly 82% of our passengers are loyal to the airline. The satisfaction among this group sits at 48%. This number reduces to 24% for non-loyal passengers, highlighting the importance of focusing on customer retention and loyalty. At the same time, the airline needs to be cautious because 82% of passengers are loyal, but satisfaction among this group still falls below 50%. Thus, improvement efforts should extend beyond retention and focus on enhancing the overall experience for existing loyal customers. 

There were other associations analysed that did not make it into the main dashboard, such as flight haul vs satisfaction outcomes. Nevertheless, with further analysis, it was found that the higher satisfaction outcomes in longer flight hauls are largely due to a higher proportion of Business Class passengers who report a much higher satisfaction rate of 69%. 

Moreover, other operational factors were considered such as the arrival delay. Whilst the data did show that even a minor delay can reduce satisfaction outcomes, it was not significant enough, and there were other more important factors to highlight in the final dashboard such as the loyalty analysis discussed earlier.
