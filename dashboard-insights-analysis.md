Centauri Airlines needs to focus on all areas of passenger experience as the average Overall Service Score is approximately 3.28 putting it into the 3.00-3.49 band. The satisfaction rate observed within this band is about 38% which is close to the overall satisfaction rate of 43%. The Overall Service Score is important as the observed satisfaction rate increases to 61% in the next band 3.50-3.99.

There are some experience areas that fall below this average for example Inflight Wi-Fi Service, Online Booking and Food and Drink. There are also other experiences that perform below average too for example Gate Location and Schedule Convenience. However, these areas may be influenced more by the airport rather than the airline itself. Therefore, the primary focus should be on weaker controllable areas such as the digital experience and catering services. 

In general, the top and bottom experience areas are consistent across the three flight classes. The service rating is usually higher in Business, followed by Economy Plus and then Economy. This is crucial as we later discover that the satisfaction rate among different classes varies greatly. Although the overall satisfaction rate is 43%, this same satisfaction rate drops to 19% for Economy passengers. This is a worthy concern as Economy passengers make up 45% of the airline’s passenger base. 

While the overall service score is consistent with different age groups, there are three age groups that represent 63% of our passenger base. These age groups are: 25-34, 35-44 and 45-54. Therefore, Centauri Airlines may want to focus future improvement efforts that align closely to the behaviour of these age groups. 

Another association that was analysed with satisfaction outcomes was loyalty. Nearly 82% of our passengers are loyal to the airline. The satisfaction among this group sits at 48%. This number reduces to 24% for non-loyal passengers highlighting the importance of focusing on customer retention and loyalty. Alternatively, the airline needs to be cautious because 82% of passengers are loyal but satisfaction within this group still falls below 50%. Thus, improvement efforts should not solely be focused on retention but rather improving the overall experience for existing loyal customers. 

There were other associations analysed that did not make it into the main dashboard for example flight haul vs satisfaction outcomes. However, with further analysis it was found that the higher satisfaction outcomes in longer flight hauls are largely due to higher proportion of Business Class passengers who report a much higher satisfaction rate of 69%. Hence, the flight haul vs satisfaction outcomes chart was replaced by a more explicit chart of flight class vs satisfaction outcomes. 

Moreover, other operational factors were also considered such as the arrival delay. Whilst the data did show that even a minor delay can reduce satisfaction outcomes, it was not significant enough and there were other more important factors to highlight in the final dashboard such as the loyalty one discussed earlier.
